# Plain language — sentence rules for every rung

In short: say who does what, in short sentences and everyday words, with the point first. Open it up, do not dumb it down — keep every fact, nuance and condition, and strip only what makes it hard to read.

Detail: the shared rules come first, then English (GOV.UK A to Z, checked 2026-11-12), then Korean.

## Shared rules (any language)

- Front-load: the most important point first in the document, each section, each paragraph and each sentence.
- One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph. If a sentence carries 2 ideas, split it.
- Active voice: say who does what. Use the passive only when the actor is unknown — and if the source should have named one, flag it as a gap.
- Be specific: the number, the name, the date. Cut vague abstractions ("a range of", "going forward", "in terms of").
- Everyday words. A defined, legal or technical term with no everyday equivalent stays, explained the first time it appears.
- Shorter applies to prose, not to structure. Headings and one-idea bullets may cost a few words; keep them when they help a reader skim. In a short answer (a few sentences), skip headings.
- No emphasis markup inside sentences: no bold or italics to shout, no ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks. A bold lead-in that labels a list item or a rung is structure, not emphasis, and is fine where the medium uses it (Slack, chat).
- Links say where they go. Use the destination page's own title when you know it, front-load the key words, and never write "click here" or "read more".
- No FAQ sections: answer the need in the content.
- Code, data tables and direct quotations keep their own conventions.

## English (GOV.UK style)

Words:

- use, not utilise or leverage; help, not facilitate or empower; work with, not collaborate, liaise or engage with
- make or provide, not deliver; about, not in relation to or with regard to; so, not in order to
- start, not commence; end, not terminate; buy, not purchase; enough, not sufficient
- solve, fix or deal with, not tackle or combat; effect on, not impact on (never impact as a verb)
- no metaphors or clichés: drive, unlock, deep dive, robust, key, ring-fence, hub, portal, landscape, ecosystem, going forward

Voice and address:

- About 15 to 20 words a sentence, never more than about 25.
- Address the reader as "you" and the organisation as "we". Use "they", not gendered pronouns. Write "disabled people", not "the disabled".
- Contractions are fine for warmth (we'll, you'll); avoid negative contractions ("cannot", not "can't") and should've, could've, would've.

Formatting:

- Sentence case everywhere: headings, titles, table headers. Capitalise only proper nouns.
- Headings: front-loaded, under about 65 characters, unique, descriptive, no full stop, dash, slash or question mark.
- Bullets: a lead-in line ending in a colon, each bullet lowercase and one idea, no "and"/"or" or semicolons at the ends, no full stop after the last (unless a bullet is a full sentence).
- Numbered steps only for a sequence followed in order; each step a full sentence with a full stop.
- Use single quotation marks for the titles of schemes or documents, not italics.
- Bold only names a literal interface element in an instruction: select Save.
- No Latin abbreviations: "for example", not "eg"; "that is", not "ie"; "such as" or "and so on", not "etc".
- "and", not "&" (except in a registered name or logo).

Numbers, dates, times:

- Write "one", then numerals from 2 upwards (2, 9, 25).
- A number that starts a sentence is written out in full ("Twelve people applied"), or the sentence is reworded.
- % with numerals (50%). £ with no decimals unless there are pence (£75, £75.50). Spell out millions and billions (£5 million).
- Ranges with "to", not a hyphen: 10 to 20, Monday to Friday, 4 to 8 June (hyphens only inside tables).
- Dates: 4 June 2026 — no comma, no "th".
- Times: 10am to 11:30am, 5:30pm — a colon, never a dot ("11.30am") or the 24-hour "1730hrs". Write "midday" and "midnight", not "12 noon" or "12pm".

## Korean (한국어)

- 결론부터. 한 문장에 생각 하나 — 길어지면 끊는다. 한 문장이 두 줄을 넘으면 거의 항상 둘이다.
- 누가 무엇을 하는지 쓴다. 능동이 기본이고, "~되어지다"·"~되어진다" 같은 이중 피동은 쓰지 않는다.
- 명사를 쌓지 말고 동사로 쓴다: "검토 진행 예정" → "검토한다", "~에 대한 확인 작업" → "~을 확인한다".
- 번역투를 걷어낸다: "~하는 것이 필요하다" → "~해야 한다", "~를 가지다" → "~가 있다", "~에 있어서" → "~에서".
- 어려운 한자어·외래어 대신 쉬운 말: "상기" → "위", "익일" → "다음 날", "활용하다" → "쓰다", "리소스" → 무엇인지 그대로(메모리·사람·돈).
- 꼭 필요한 기술 용어는 그대로 두고 처음 나올 때 한 번 풀어 쓴다: "롤백(배포 이전 상태로 되돌리기)".
- 숫자는 아라비아 숫자와 단위를 붙여 쓴다: 3개, 15분, 2.5배. 시각은 "오후 5시 30분" 또는 "17:30"으로 한 문서 안에서 하나만 쓴다. 범위는 "10~20개" 또는 "10개에서 20개".
- 강조하려고 굵게·느낌표·대문자를 쓰지 않는다. 문장 순서가 강조다.
- 다 쓴 글이 딱딱하면 `zkorean` 에이전트로 어색한 부분만 고친다 — 뜻·수치·고유명사는 그대로 둔다.

## Self-check

- Is the single most important thing first — in the document and in every section?
- Could a reader outside the domain follow every sentence on the first read?
- Is every sentence active, short and one idea?
- Any emphasis markup, jargon, Latin abbreviation or marketing word left?
- Sentence case, descriptive headings, link text that names its destination?
- Numbers, dates and times in the forms above — colon in times, no sentence starting with a numeral?
- Could you cut any more words without losing meaning? If yes, cut them.
